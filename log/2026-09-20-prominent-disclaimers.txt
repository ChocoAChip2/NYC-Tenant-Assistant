2026-09-20 -- Prominent disclaimers: top banner, per-message note, referral promise
===================================================================================

WHAT CHANGED

1. A persistent amber banner under the header on /chat and /settings,
   carrying branding.TOP_DISCLAIMER:

     "Disclaimer: In the scenario in which you are actively seeking legal
      assistance, please message our chatbot to let them know -- they will
      provide a list of sources that you may contact in your area. ST does
      NOT provide legal advice and should never be used for that specific
      purpose. Thank you for your understanding."

   Written by the product owner. Reproduced as given except for one
   spelling fix ("advise" -> "advice") and one em dash for readability.

2. branding.PER_MESSAGE_DISCLAIMER ("General information, not legal
   advice.") under every assistant reply, on both render paths -- the
   Jinja loop for stored history AND renderMessage() in JS for the reply
   that just arrived. Updating only one would have meant the note appears
   on reload but not on the message the tenant is actually reading.

3. Rule 7 in ai_service.py's system prompt, interpolating
   branding.HELP_RESOURCES in full with phone numbers, so that asking for
   legal help produces the actual list rather than "consult an attorney".

WHY 3 EXISTS AT ALL

The banner makes a promise: say you need legal assistance and you will
get a list of contacts. Nothing in the product previously guaranteed
that. A banner promising something the product does not do is worse than
no banner -- it is a reliance the site invited. So the prompt rule and a
test class (ReferralPromiseTests) now hold the banner, the prompt and the
resource list together; the tests fail if any one of them drifts.

DESIGN NOTE -- VISUAL WEIGHT IS INVERTED AGAINST REPETITION

The banner appears once and is loud: --warning on --warning-tint, a 4px
left rule, a round "!" glyph. The per-message note repeats every turn and
is deliberately quiet: 11px, --muted, italic, a small amber dot.

This is the same concern that shaped the escalation triggers in the
previous batch. Repetition is what destroys a disclaimer -- people stop
seeing the thing they have scrolled past twenty times. Making the
repeated layer quiet and the once-per-session layer loud is the way to
have both without the loud one going stale.

The banner is not dismissible, and a test asserts there is no close
button inside it.
