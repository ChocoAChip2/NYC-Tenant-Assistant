Deployment Summary — Renamed to SideKick Tidbit, ST monogram, standing legal
disclaimer on every page, and a public Learn More / support page
Date: 2026-09-20

Requirements (from conversation):
1. "We are going to name the site 'SideKick Tidbit' and ST is going to be
   made into a logo."
2. "Notice how some AI's give information but they also include a smaller
   disclaimer. Please have disclaimer plugged in for things that we don't
   want to be held liable for. But we should still explicitly say in our
   actual responses, besides the smaller outline at the bottom, that
   disclaims that we don't provide legal advice."
3. "Put a pin in a 'Learn More' page which we can reference in our website
   that leads directly to our support/info page."

Two things were confirmed before building, because guessing either one
wrong meant redoing the work:
- How often the assistant itself should say "not legal advice" -> ONLY when
  the stakes are real. This matters because it pulls directly against an
  earlier instruction ("make responses dynamic, not the typical response"):
  a disclaimer line on every single reply is exactly how boilerplate creeps
  back in.
- Whether to apply the rename now or wait for the logo -> apply now, with a
  placeholder mark.

Part 1 -- The rename (branding.py, all templates, routes.py):

Every user-facing "NYC Tenant Assistant" is now "SideKick Tidbit": page
titles, the auth-card brand line, the chat header, the composer
placeholder, and the chat-history export header.

The name is NOT written into any template. It comes from branding.py via a
context processor, and a test asserts no template hardcodes it. The reason
is this branch: the name was hardcoded in six files, and renaming it meant
finding thirteen separate strings. The next rename is one edit.

The ST mark reuses the accent-colored rounded square the auth pages already
had, with the monogram as white text inside it. It is an interim mark, not
a final logo -- swapping in real artwork means replacing one element rather
than reworking a layout. Added to the chat header and the settings header
too, which previously had no brand at all.

Part 2 -- The standing disclaimer (branding.py + every template):

Every page now carries, in small muted type:

  "SideKick Tidbit gives general information, not legal advice. Learn more"

Placement follows each page's shape: under the card on the auth pages, last
in <main> on settings, and on the chat page under the composer below the
character counter -- the placement ChatGPT and Claude both use, which is
the convention the request was modelled on. On chat it sits outside the
conversation/greeting branch so the greeting state shows it too.

WHY THE WORDING LIVES IN PYTHON: this app has no shared layout; every
template is standalone and repeats its own styling. That is a deliberate
choice for CSS and a bad one for legal text. Six hand-copied disclaimers
drift, and the one that drifts is the one that gets quoted back at you. So
the words live in branding.py once, and a test asserts no template
hardcodes the phrase "not legal advice" (learn_more.html excepted -- that
phrase is its section heading).

A second test asserts the disclaimer string actually contains "not legal
advice", guarding against someone later softening it into something
friendlier that no longer does the job it exists to do.

Part 3 -- When the assistant says it out loud (ai_service.py):

The footer is the standing notice. The assistant states it in its own reply
only when the stakes are real. The triggers are defined in
branding.ESCALATION_TRIGGERS and interpolated into the system prompt, so
the product decision about WHEN sits beside the words themselves:

  - an active or threatened court case, including an eviction proceeding
  - a deadline that has passed or is about to
  - a document they have already signed, or are being asked to sign
  - anything involving money they could lose or owe
  - a decision that is hard to reverse once made

In those replies it must say plainly that it is not a lawyer, point to a
housing attorney or free helpline, and NOT bury it at the end -- someone
about to miss a court date should not have to read to the bottom.

The prompt also explicitly keeps the other half: no disclaimer on routine
factual questions. Both halves are tested. Stapling the same sentence onto
"what temperature must my landlord keep the heat at" is what trains people
to skip past it on the message where it actually matters, which is the
failure mode this design is avoiding.

Part 4 -- /learn-more (new route + template):

Public on purpose, no login required. Someone deciding whether to trust
this with their housing situation should be able to read what it claims to
be before handing over an email address, and someone who has just been told
something alarming should be able to find a lawyer without authenticating
first.

Contents, all driven from branding.py: the full four-paragraph disclaimer
(not a law firm, no attorney-client relationship, the AI can be wrong,
filled forms are drafts you remain responsible for); an explicit
can/cannot list; five free or low-cost help resources (NYC Tenant Helpline,
Housing Court Answers, NYC Right to Counsel, NY State HCR, HPD complaints);
and a plain-language summary of the data handling already built. Outbound
links use target="_blank" with rel="noopener noreferrer" so a tenant
mid-conversation doesn't lose it by tapping a helpline.

Part 5 -- A gap this surfaced in the test setup:

Adding the context processor immediately broke a test, and the reason was
worth more than the fix: create_app() registered the branding, but the ~10
tests that build a bare Flask app around main_bp never call create_app().
Those tests were rendering pages with an EMPTY product name and NO
disclaimer, and would have stayed green about it -- the page still comes
out, just unbranded.

This is the same shape as the earlier csrf_token() problem, so it got the
same fix: branding.register(app) is a shared function called by both
create_app() and the test helper. The helper itself was renamed from
csrf_test_support.disable_csrf to app_test_support.configure_test_app,
because it now does two things and a name saying only "disable csrf" would
be the sort of quiet lie this codebase's docs exist to prevent.
