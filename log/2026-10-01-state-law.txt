2026-10-01  State tenant law in the legal library (NY Senate Open Legislation API)

What
- tools/corpus/nys.py reads New York State law from the NY Senate Open
  Legislation API. It uses one request per law (`?full=true` returns the
  whole tree with text) and takes the article or title each source names.
- Five sources, counted from the live API:
  - Good Cause Eviction (RPL Art. 6-A): 9 sections
  - RPL Art. 7, landlord and tenant: 55 (235-b habitability, 223-b
    retaliation, 226-c notice of increase/non-renewal, 235-e receipts,
    238-a fees, ...)
  - RPAPL Art. 7, summary proceedings/evictions: 32
  - RPAPL Art. 7-A, tenants' proceedings and 7A administrators: 15
  - GOL Art. 7 Title 1, security deposits: 7
- Same gates and write path as the city sources (90% minimum count,
  anchors, the token-checked corpus_* RPCs). Rows are jurisdiction NYS.
  The official URL is the section's nysenate.gov page.

Accuracy rules
- The adapter was written from real responses captured with the key, not
  from the docs (the earlier plan's rule).
- The API prints every dated version of a provision. RPAPL § 711 and RPL
  § 226-c carry both their current Good Cause text ("Effective until June
  15, 2034") and their post-sunset text ("Effective June 15, 2034"). Only
  the version in force on the day of the refresh is stored as law; the
  other is noted as not shown. A quarterly refresh after 2034 will pick
  up the other version by itself.
- Publisher notes ("NB Repealed June 15, 2034") are kept as notes, never
  as law text.
- The key is read from the environment, never logged, and scrubbed from
  any error message.

Running it
- Locally: NYSENATE_API_KEY plus the usual SUPABASE_URL, SUPABASE_KEY and
  CORPUS_INGEST_TOKEN.
- GitHub Actions: the workflow passes secrets.NYSENATE_API_KEY. Until the
  owner adds that secret, scheduled runs skip the state sources, say so in
  the summary, and refresh the city sources as before.
